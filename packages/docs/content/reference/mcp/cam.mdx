The `cam` tool pack turns a vcad document into a CNC milling program, and
refuses to hand one over that does not make the part.

These are gated behind a [tool pack](#enabling-the-pack), so enable `cam` before
calling them.

## The safety contract

Three rules, and they are the reason this pack exists rather than a
`generate_toolpath` call.

**Verify by default.** `cam_job` posts the program and then replays it against
the part it is meant to make — every move, against the outline, the stock, the
declared tabs and the machine's travel — before returning it.

**Blocked means do not cut.** If any error-severity check objects, the result
carries `blocked: true`, the names of the checks that objected, and **no G-code
anywhere in it**. Not a flag beside a program you could still run: there is
nothing to export.

**Physical predictions are provisional.** Cycle time, feeds, speeds and cutter
fit are computed from geometry and published chiploads. They are predictions
until a part comes off the machine and is measured. `cam_gear` closes that loop
explicitly: measure over pins, pass the reading back, get the cutter offset for
the next part.

## The workflow

```
create_cad_loon  →  cam_outline  →  cam_fit  →  cam_recommend_feeds  →  cam_job
                         ↓                                                 ↓
                   is it prismatic?                            blocked, or G-code
                   which holes, how big?                       at an artifact URL
```

`cam_outline` names the contours it finds — `"outer"`, `"hole:0"`, `"hole:1"` —
and `cam_fit` and `cam_job` accept those names against the same `document_id`.
The coordinates never have to travel through the conversation.

Every tool takes `detail: "full"` to attach the arrays it otherwise summarises:
contour points, the complete verification report, the G-code text inline.

## cam_outline

Sections a document part at a Z height into the closed contours a milling job
cuts.

### Which mesh it sections, and why that matters

A part carries two meshes' worth of geometry: the *export* mesh the app renders
and prints, and — where the root is still a B-rep — the solid itself. The export
mesh is `tessellate_brep` followed by `repair_export_mesh`, which closes the
boundary for printing and ray-tracing by moving vertices onto their analytic
carriers. A plane through the moved region then cuts a slightly different shape.

Measured on the stator (2026-09-17): the export mesh sections with tears up to
**0.4 mm** where tangent fillets meet, while the raw tessellation of the same
B-rep sections cleanly to **0.005 mm**. A CAM contour is a wall the cutter
follows, so 0.4 mm is not a rounding difference — it is a quarter of a slot
mouth.

So `cam_outline` sections the **raw tessellation** whenever there is a B-rep,
and says which it used in `mesh_source`. A part with no B-rep behind it — an
imported mesh — can only be sectioned as it stands, and the result says so
rather than letting you assume the better path was taken.

A section that does not close is **refused**, with the gap positions, rather
than quietly healed: an open section means the solid is torn there, and the
outline is the symptom.

<Param name="document_id" type="string" required={true}>
CAD session holding the part.
</Param>

<Param name="part" type="string" required={false}>
Root part id or exact name. Optional when the document holds one part.
</Param>

<Param name="z" type="number" required={false}>
Height to section at, mm. Omit to section at the part's own mid-height.
</Param>

<Param name="heal_tolerance" type="number" required={false}>
Close gaps narrower than this when chaining the section, mm (default `1e-3`).
Raising it hides a torn solid rather than fixing one.
</Param>

### Returns

`mesh_source`, the section height and the part's Z range (which is the stock
thickness to buy), one entry per region with its hole count and area, every
hole that fits a circle with its fitted diameter and fit error, and a
**prismatic verdict** — whether sections at several heights agree, and by how
many millimetres they do not. A 2.5D contour job only describes a part whose
shape does not change with depth; a cone is not one, and the report says so in
millimetres rather than as a boolean.

## cam_fit

Does this cutter physically fit this contour?

Answers the number of inside corners it cannot reach, how much metal that
leaves (mm²), how far the worst of it stands off, and the largest cutter that
would still fit. Run it before `cam_job`: a cutter that does not fit does not
fail — it quietly leaves wall standing in every corner.

Geometry only. It says nothing about deflection or chatter, so treat a pass as
necessary and not sufficient.

## cam_recommend_feeds

Feeds, speeds, stepdown and stepover for a material and cutter on a machine of a
given rigidity class.

Includes the **dial position** to set by hand whenever the spindle is a trim
router, because on those the `S` word does nothing and a recommendation that
only said "13 500 rpm" would be unusable.

Pass `settings` with numbers you already have and they are checked against the
table instead. Pass `list: true` for the materials.

## cam_job

Post a whole job — several operations, several tools, one spindle start per
tool — and verify it before handing it over.

Operation kinds: `face`, `pocket`, `contour_outside`, `contour_inside`, `drill`,
`helical_bore`. Inside features are sequenced before the outside profile that
frees the part, whatever order they were given in.

The checks that can block: **gouge** (the cutter enters the part),
**material_left** (wall the job never reaches), **rapids** (a rapid below the
stock top with XY motion), **depth** (past the stock with nothing declared
beneath it), **tabs** (the tabs asked for are not the tabs standing),
**envelope** (outside the machine's travel, given a work offset), **loose** (the
job frees a piece before it is finished), **plunges** (a straight plunge with a
tool that cannot cut on its centre).

Two fields worth knowing:

<Param name="tab_positions" type="array" required={false}>
Where the tabs go, as fractions of the way round the cutter's loop, instead of
spacing `tabs` of them evenly. Nominal — the kernel settles each one onto the
nearest stretch that runs straight, and `tab_placement` in the result reports
where each ended up, how much metal it leaves and how far apart they are.
</Param>

<Param name="options.placement" type="object" required={false}>
`{ dx, dy, rotation_deg }` — where the part sits on the stock, rotated about the
stock origin and then shifted. Moves every operation **and** the part the oracle
replays against, so a job on skewed stock still verifies against the skewed
part. A per-operation `placement` moves that operation's geometry alone, and the
result says so.
</Param>

### Returns

On a pass: the G-code as an artifact URL with its size and SHA-256, the check
summary, the operation table with per-operation seconds, the swept envelope, the
deepest Z reached, and where the tabs landed.

On a refusal: `blocked: true`, `blocked_by`, the checks with their worst values,
and `gcode: null`.

## cam_verify_gcode

Replay a program you already have — off disk, out of another CAM package, edited
by hand — against the part it is meant to make.

This answers the question you cannot answer by reading the file: does it cut the
right side of every wall, stay out of the part, reach the right depth, leave the
tabs it claims, and stay inside the machine? A pass is a 2.5D geometric replay;
it does not model deflection, chatter, or a machine that is not where it says it
is.

## cam_gear

Exact involute gear geometry for cutting on a mill: the tooth-space contour the
named cutter actually leaves, the reachability check (a cutter too fat for the
space is refused rather than cutting a different tooth), the over-pins dimension
to measure, contact ratio, and planetary-train meshing.

Pass `measured` with the pin diameter you used and it returns the cutter offset
that reading implies for the next part. That loop is how a predicted tooth
thickness becomes a known one.

## Enabling the pack

```
set_tool_packs({ enable: ["cam"] })
```

or set `VCAD_MCP_PACKS=cam,...` in the server environment. `list_tool_packs`
shows what is on.

## One implementation, three front ends

These tools, the native app's C ABI and the browser's WASM all call the same
crate — `vcad-cam-api`. That is deliberate: an agent and the app must never be
able to disagree about whether a job is safe to run, and a second
implementation of "is this blocked?" would be a second answer.
