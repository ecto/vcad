Compression Math Survey

Six chapters on the mathematics behind compression, read for one purpose: designing a general compression library on tang's expression IR, and deciding whether a Hutter Prize attempt is worth it.

The picture in one paragraph

Compressed size is cross-entropy plus the cost of the model, so every compressor is a predictor with a bill attached. The chapters split into three ways of predicting and one shared set of plumbing. Structure finds low-rank shift structure (Hankel matrices, tensor trains, weighted automata). Statistics mixes many models online with regret guarantees. Programs search for a short generator. Underneath all three sit quantization, entropy coding and one hard rule: the coding path must be bit-exact on every backend.

Structure

One theorem in three forms: Kronecker for signals, Fliess for strings, QTT ranks for multiscale grids. Every decoder is a product of small matrices, which tang lowers to Add and Mul.

Statistics

Excess file size equals the predictor's regret. CTW, Pitman–Yor and fixed-share mixing give exact Bayesian answers; PAQ-style logistic mixing is online gradient descent on log loss.

Programs

Kolmogorov complexity is the ideal; Levin search and the speed prior make it resource-bounded. Symbolic regression and CAD program inference are the practical forms.

What this means for a Hutter attempt

The record entry (fx2-cmix-transformer, 100,424,672 bytes, 2026) already mixes a pretrained 6M-parameter 4-bit transformer with online context models. The math points at four levers.

  1. Find the pretrain/online split. Stop adding stored weights when one more byte of weights saves less than one byte of early-learning loss. No one has published this ablation on enwik9.
  2. Code the weights by information, not uniformly. Spend precision by Fisher information, or use singular learning theory where the Fisher matrix is degenerate. Use a randomized Hadamard rotation plus E8 or trellis quantization (the QuIP#/QTIP recipe), or relative entropy coding at about KL bits.
  3. Train the transformer for the mixture. Its job is to predict what the context models miss, so train through a simulated mixer.
  4. Upgrade the mixer. Online Newton Step-style second-order updates, and fixed-share switching across the roughly 240k articles.

Chapters

Chapter 1

MDL and the cost of models

  • Prequential codes never send weights: the decoder is the training loop.
  • MML ties weight precision to 1/√Fisher.
  • Bits-back and relative entropy coding price weights at roughly KL(q‖p).
  • New pointers: requential coding, MDL with singular learning theory.

Chapter 2

Universal prediction and mixing

  • Parameters cost about (k/2)·log₂ n bits each.
  • CTW mixes doubly exponentially many tree models at O(D) per symbol.
  • Kneser–Ney is approximate hierarchical Pitman–Yor inference.
  • Fixed share and switching handle changing sources cheaply.
docs/compression-math-survey/02-universal-prediction-mixing.md
Chapter 3

Hankel, tensor trains, automata

  • QTT ranks: exponential 1, sinusoid 2, degree-p polynomial p+1.
  • Tensorized trains match wavelet rates on Besov classes (Ali–Nouy).
  • A weighted automaton is a stationary MPS.
  • QTT plus a sparse Walsh residual as a two-layer codec.
docs/compression-math-survey/03-hankel-tensor-networks.md
Chapter 4

Rate–distortion, quantization, coding

  • Dithered quantization is exactly signal plus uniform noise, the identity behind the training proxy.
  • Dither must come from a counter-based integer PRNG.
  • Interleaved rANS maps to GPU warps.
  • Perfect realism costs at most 2× the MSE.
docs/compression-math-survey/04-rate-distortion-quantization-coding.md
Chapter 5

Algorithmic information

  • Every compressor is an upper bound on Kolmogorov complexity.
  • The Hutter Prize is a time-capped Levin-style objective.
  • Levin's 2^−length time allocation orders expression search.
  • CAD program inference plus differentiable fitting gives a mesh codec.
docs/compression-math-survey/05-algorithmic-information.md
Chapter 6

Structure, floors and symmetry

  • Mutual information in text decays as a power law (Lin & Tegmark); block measurements suggest near volume-law scaling, which favors attention over plain trees.
  • String attractors: LZ77, BWT runs, grammars and macro schemes all approximate one quantity, γ; δ is a linear-time proxy.
  • Pesin and Brudno: chaotic trajectories cost Σλ⁺ bits per second, so replay compression needs keyframes about every 1/λ_max.
  • Gavish–Donoho thresholds and symmetry savings of log₂|G| bits.
